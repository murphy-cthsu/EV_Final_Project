## TL;DR (read this first)

1. **The contribution is a 3-component joint system, not "better ARAP."** Articulation-aware piecewise-rigid ARAP (Component 1) is the *direct* articulation mechanism; ARAP-energy-based supervision gating (Component 2) and frequency-domain motion curriculum (Component 3) are *protective* mechanisms that keep Component 1 from being washed out by the high-freq drift / hallucinated frames characteristic of generative-video supervision. The novelty is the intersection — RigGS does Component 1 alone but only because their supervision is clean captured video; the diffusion-supervised cluster (ViDAR, 4D-Fly, DIFF4SPLAT) has no drift-rejection mechanism. See §2 for the precise component → pathology mapping.

2. **W1 gate passed for Component 1.** SC-GS-default trains stably on 4 D-NeRF articulated scenes; quantitative evidence that the deformation field has a structured residual that scales with articulation complexity: **periphery-vs-core reconstruction error ratio** tracks scene articulation monotonically (jumpingjacks **×2.28** → hellwarrior ×1.35 → standup ×1.14 → bouncingballs **×1.03** non-articulated control). Radial-error profile ramps **5–6×** on jumpingjacks, flat on bouncingballs. This isolates Component 1's failure mode but does *not* exercise Components 2 or 3 (clean multi-view supervision — no drift to gate against).

3. **SV4D-supervised pipeline implemented and CPU-tested end-to-end.** This is the experimental tier (Tier 2 in §4) where Components 2 and 3 begin to flex — generative supervision exposes drift the gating + curriculum components are designed to absorb. 117/117 pytest cases pass including the CPU smoke that runs prepare → fake-SV4D → convert → SC-GS dataset_reader parses. Awaiting first GPU run.

4. **Three dataset tiers (§4):** Tier 1 multi-view D-NeRF (W1 done — Component 1 only); Tier 2 SV4D-supervised D-NeRF (W3 — the joint system flexes); Tier 3 DyCheck real monocular (W3 — real-world existence proof). Each tier tests a specific subset of the contribution.

5. **Methodological honesty (§3 & §6).** The W1 numbers don't yet prove uniform-ARAP is the *cause* of the failure — that's the W2 discriminating ablations. ## 1. The 3-component contribution (re-grounded)

Per `docs/vwm_framing.md` §3 — three components, each addressing a *different* pathology of generative-video supervision. **Component 1 directly improves articulation; Components 2 and 3 protect Component 1's signal from being corrupted by generative drift.** None of the three is novel alone; the joint system is.

| # | Component | Targets | Why it survives the audit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **SAM-2-grounded piecewise-rigid ARAP** — anisotropic per-edge λ_intra ≫ λ_inter from SAM-2 hierarchical part masks on the *static* input frame | Joint-region blur, limb-end ghosting; the **W1-measured** failure mode (×2.28 periphery ratio on jumpingjacks) | RigGS does Component 1 with skeleton extraction → fails on generative video. VideoArtGS does it with motion-clustering → fails on drifty supervision. We use SAM-2 on the *static* image, so part structure is fixed before any motion noise enters. |
| **2** | **ARAP-energy-based supervision gating** — per-frame photometric weight `exp(−α(t) · E_t)` from lifted sparse trajectories | **Geometrically implausible (hallucinated) supervision frames** | The diffusion-supervised cluster (ViDAR, 4D-Fly, DIFF4SPLAT, CAT4D, 4Diffusion) has no physical drift-rejection mechanism beyond appearance masks. Ours is the only physically-grounded supervision-quality signal in the cluster. |
| **3** | **Frequency-domain motion curriculum** — Fourier-band mask on the deformation MLP's temporal positional encoding | **High-frequency hallucinations** (per-frame appearance flicker, micro-jitter on stationary regions) | Coarse-to-fine training exists for static 3DGS; nobody has applied it as a band-mask on the *temporal* PE of a 4D deformation field. Prevents the MLP from over-fitting noisy high-freq components of the SV4D output. |

### How each component relates to articulation

Important precision — only Component 1 *adds articulation structure*. Components 2 and 3 *protect* the articulation signal from generative-noise corruption.

| Component | Role for articulation | Without it (clean supervision) | Without it (generative supervision) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **DIRECT.** Encodes "this is one rigid part, this is another, the boundary is the joint." | Uniform ARAP. Field smoothly bends; joints don't localize. (The W1 finding.) | Same as multi-view case. |
| 2 | **PROTECTIVE.** Down-weights frames where lifted trajectories aren't near-rigid. | No effect (uniform weight on clean frames). | Field learns hallucinated articulation — limbs in physically impossible positions baked into the deformation MLP. |
| 3 | **PROTECTIVE.** Locks out high-freq channels of the temporal PE during early training. | Marginal effect (longer convergence). | Deformation MLP absorbs per-frame jitter as "real" articulation — joints vibrate instead of cleanly rotating. |

**Implication for the W1 evidence.** Multi-view D-NeRF doesn't exercise Components 2 or 3 — the supervision is clean, so gating returns uniform weight and curriculum is just a slower-convergence regularizer. The 2.28× periphery ratio measures **Component 1's failure mode in isolation**. The bigger headline numbers will appear at **Tier 2** (SV4D-supervised), where Components 2 and 3 start doing useful work.

## 3. W1 headline number (Component 1 only, multi-view supervision)

Per-pixel reconstruction error on all 20 test frames of each scene. Foreground mask = GT-luminance > 0.05 (D-NeRF black background). Core mask = foreground morphologically eroded by 30 pixels. Periphery = foreground \ core.

| scene | core err  | periphery err | **periphery / core** | what this scene tests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| jumpingjacks  | 0.0129 ± 0.0055 | **0.0294 ± 0.0093** | **×2.28** | Full-body cyclical articulation — 4D-Fly's named failure mode |
| hellwarrior   | 0.0156 ± 0.0097 | 0.0210 ± 0.0037 | ×1.35 | Localized articulation + rotating weapon |
| standup       | 0.0136 ± 0.0091 | 0.0155 ± 0.0039 | ×1.14 | Gradual upward motion, mostly torso-coherent |
| bouncingballs | 0.0133 ± 0.0074 | 0.0136 ± 0.0062 | **×1.03** | Rigid balls, no kinematic chain — **control** |

The control sits at unity. The articulation-difficulty rank order matches the rank order of pf σ from the cross-scene boxplot (jumpingjacks pf σ 3.49, bouncingballs 2.37, etc.) — two independent signals agree.

## 4. What W1 evidence does and does not support

**Supports (defensibly):**
- The SC-GS-default deformation field has a *structured* spatial residual that scales monotonically with scene articulation complexity (jumpingjacks → bouncingballs control).
- The residual concentrates at object periphery (limb extremities) and is consistent with the predicted failure mode of uniform-rigidity ARAP on articulated kinematic chains.
- The non-articulated control (bouncingballs) sits at ratio ×1.03 — the signal is articulation-specific, not high-motion-in-general.

**Does NOT yet support:**
- That **uniform-rigidity ARAP specifically** is the *cause*. Same residuals could come from insufficient node-graph density at limb tips (node_num=512), insufficient iters (30K vs SC-GS's 40K paper number), or hyper_dim=8 being too small. **The discriminating ablation lives in W2.**
- That **Component 1 fixes it.** The W2 ablation matrix is the test.
- That **Components 2 and 3 matter at all.** Multi-view D-NeRF doesn't have the drift Components 2 and 3 are designed to filter, so they are effectively dormant in Tier 1. The Tier 2 (SV4D-supervised) experiment is where they activate.
- That **D-NeRF reveals deployment ill-posedness.** D-NeRF gives full multi-view supervision at every timestep — the reconstruction problem is well-posed by construction. The structured residual we measure is under the *easiest possible supervision regime*; it is a *lower bound* on the failure under SV4D-generated supervision.

Reviewers will reasonably ask "why is 40.85 PSNR a failure?". The honest answer: the residual is structured (periphery, articulation-specific) and predicts a worse failure under the deployment regime, but the multi-view D-NeRF number alone is not the right basis for the failure claim. **The SV4D-supervised D-NeRF (Tier 2) and DyCheck (Tier 3) rows are where the full 3-component contribution is actually exercised.**

### Tier 1 (W1 done): ablation matrix to close the Component 1 claim

The W1 finding (×2.28 periphery ratio on jumpingjacks) is suggestive but not yet causal. The W2 ablation matrix turns it into a defensible "Component 1 fixes the failure" claim. Each row rules out a specific alternative explanation:

| Ablation | Targets which alternative explanation? | Predicted outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **A. shuffled part labels** (Component 1 active, SAM-2 ids randomized) | "Any extra regularizer helps" | Wins should collapse to baseline. If shuffled labels still improve PSNR, our claim is wrong. |
| **B. higher-capacity uniform baseline** (node_num 512 → 1024, no Component 1) | "Just needed more capacity" | Modest uniform gain across all scenes including bouncingballs. Does **not** preferentially help articulated scenes. |
| **C. Component 1 (real SAM-2 labels)** | The claim itself | Wins concentrate on jumpingjacks > hellwarrior > standup; ~zero gain on bouncingballs. |
| **D. Component 1 + longer training** (40K iters, matching SC-GS paper) | "Our hook is just slow convergence" | Component 1 at 30K should match or beat baseline at 40K. |

Plus the diagnostic: **per-radial-bin Δ-PSNR** vs. SC-GS-default. Wins should localize to the outer 25% of normalized radius. If wins are spatially uniform, the framing is wrong.

## 6. Honest weaknesses, with fixability assessment

Four weaknesses to flag for tomorrow. Of these, **only #4 has a fundamental ceiling**; the other three are engineering or scoping decisions with clear paths forward.

| # | Weakness | Kind | Can it be overcome? | Cost / how |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | "Single-image" framing reframed to "single short video" under Option A — SV4D 2.0 architecturally requires a video input | Architectural | **Yes, two routes** | (a) Add SVD / CogVideoX-I2V in front: ~3 days engineering + 10–20% PSNR cost from cascaded drift — but **Components 2+3 are exactly the right tools for that drift**, so the weakness partly becomes an opportunity. (b) Reframe to "single observation = one short clip"; zero engineering cost, slight rhetorical concession that embodied robotics audiences accept. **Current recommendation: (b) for the paper, (a) as future work / appendix.** |
| **2** | The 3-component story is ambitious for 4 weeks (Components 2 and 3 have stub code but no SV4D-supervised validation yet) | Project management | **Yes, by triage** | If Component 3 doesn't show clear gains in Tier 2 ablation, demote it to appendix and ship the 2-component story (1 + 2 = articulation + drift-rejection). The W2 ablation matrix is structured so we'll know which components are pulling weight by Tier 2 day 3. |
| **3** | Inter-part angular consistency metric is new and un-validated against community benchmarks | Engineering | **Yes, easily — 1–2 days** | Compute the metric on (a) RigGS published outputs or our reproduction, (b) MoSca, (c) our SC-GS-default. Sanity check: ranks methods consistently with PSNR on static parts; discriminates piecewise-rigid from elastic on moving parts. If it fails the sanity check, pivot to PA-CD or trajectory-EPE. |
| **4** | Component 2 (gating) quality is bounded by trajectory-lifting quality on the SV4D output | Partially architectural | **Refinable but capped** | Engineering wins: better optical flow (RAFT-3D, GMFlow, CoTracker), MoGe / Depth Anything V3 for 3D lifting, iterative refinement from the trained 4DGS itself. **Fundamental ceiling:** if SV4D produces "consistently wrong" output (coherent-looking but physically-wrong limb position across all 21 frames × multiple views), no trajectory lifter can detect it — the energy will be low and gating will keep the bad frames. This failure mode is rare in practice (diffusion models tend to be *inconsistently* wrong) but it's the one weakness that doesn't close with more engineering. |

**Additional scoping risks (less load-bearing but worth airing):**

- **RigGS published 40.82 on jumpingjacks.** Our SC-GS-default sits at 40.85. If the full hook only moves us to ~41.5 on multi-view D-NeRF, we don't beat their published PSNR. Counter-narrative: (a) we win on inter-part angular consistency (metric they don't report), (b) we operate in a regime they can't (generative-video supervision), (c) Tier 2 numbers should show much larger gaps than Tier 1.
- **Tier 1 may not visibly win** — the Component 1 effect under clean multi-view supervision could be PSNR-noise-floor small (because uniform ARAP already does *most* of the work when supervision is dense). Mitigation: report the radial-profile delta and inter-part angular consistency, not aggregate PSNR. The Tier 2 numbers will carry the headline.
- **Compute plan** — lab box has handled W1 cleanly. Tier 1 W2 budget ~50 lab-hr (3-way parallel ≈ 17 hr wall). Tier 2 SC-GS training stays on lab; SV4D inference likely needs RunPod H100 ($15–20). All within budget.

## TL;DR (read this first)

1. **W1 gate passed.** SC-GS-default trains stably on 4 D-NeRF scenes (jumpingjacks, hellwarrior, bouncingballs, standup) at 30K iters in ~18 min critical path on the 3-card lab box. PSNR within 2.2 of the SC-GS paper's 40K-iter number on jumpingjacks — env is healthy.

2. **The failure mode is real and quantifiable.** Across the four scenes, **periphery-vs-core reconstruction error ratio tracks articulation complexity monotonically**:
   - jumpingjacks ×2.28 → hellwarrior ×1.35 → standup ×1.14 → bouncingballs ×1.03.
   - bouncingballs (the non-articulated control with four rigid balls) is statistically indistinguishable between core and periphery. The signal is articulation-specific, not high-motion-in-general.

3. **The radial error profile is the discriminating chart.** On jumpingjacks, mean per-pixel error grows from ~0.015 at the body centroid to ~0.085 at limb extremities — a **5–6× radial ramp**. On bouncingballs it's flat through 80% of normalized radius with only a thin silhouette-boundary spike. This is the strongest single argument that uniform-rigidity ARAP under-fits articulated kinematic chains.

4. **SV4D-supervised SC-GS pipeline is implemented and CPU-tested end-to-end.** SV4D 2.0 weights downloaded (24 GB, both variants); upstream `generative-models` repo cloned; real `SV4D2Adapter` wraps their `simple_video_sample_4d2.py` via subprocess; converter writes SC-GS-readable scene dirs; full pipeline driver is idempotent and runs prepare→fake-SV4D→convert→SC-GS-reader-parses in ~10 s on CPU. 11 new pytest cases all pass. Awaiting first GPU run.

5. **Methodological honesty.** Multi-view D-NeRF is *the controlled comparison* benchmark, not *the ill-posedness benchmark*. The deployment regime (single GT frame + SV4D-generated supervision) hasn't been stress-tested yet. Section 4 below proposes the W2 protocol that actually tests our claim.

## 2. The headline number, derived

Per-pixel reconstruction error on all 20 test frames of each scene. Foreground mask = GT-luminance > 0.05 (D-NeRF black background). Core mask = foreground morphologically eroded by 30 pixels. Periphery = foreground \ core.

| scene | core err  | periphery err | **periphery / core** | what this scene tests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| jumpingjacks  | 0.0129 ± 0.0055 | **0.0294 ± 0.0093** | **×2.28** | Full-body cyclical articulation — 4D-Fly's named failure mode |
| hellwarrior   | 0.0156 ± 0.0097 | 0.0210 ± 0.0037 | ×1.35 | Localized articulation + rotating weapon |
| standup       | 0.0136 ± 0.0091 | 0.0155 ± 0.0039 | ×1.14 | Gradual upward motion, mostly torso-coherent |
| bouncingballs | 0.0133 ± 0.0074 | 0.0136 ± 0.0062 | **×1.03** | Rigid balls, no kinematic chain — **control** |

The control sits at unity. The articulation-difficulty rank order matches the rank order of pf σ from the cross-scene boxplot (jumpingjacks pf σ 3.49, bouncingballs 2.37, etc.) — two independent signals agree.

Source: `scripts/spatial_error_analysis.py`, `outputs/_cross_scene_failure/spatial_error_summary.json`.

## 3. What we DO NOT yet claim

This is load-bearing for the W2 framing and worth raising in the discussion explicitly.

**The W1 evidence shows:**
- The deformation field is unstable in localized regions, in a way that scales with scene articulation complexity.
- This is consistent with the predicted failure mode of uniform-rigidity ARAP on articulated kinematic chains.

**The W1 evidence DOES NOT show:**

- That **uniform-rigidity ARAP specifically** is the cause. The same residuals could come from insufficient node-graph density at limb tips (node_num=512), insufficient iters (30K vs SC-GS's 40K paper number), or hyper_dim=8 being too small. The discriminating ablation is W2.
- That **part-aware ARAP fixes it.** The W2 ablation matrix is the test.
- That **D-NeRF reveals the deployment ill-posedness.** D-NeRF gives full multi-view supervision at every timestep — the reconstruction problem is well-posed by construction. The fan-trails we measure are under the *easiest possible supervision regime*; they are a lower bound on the failure under SV4D-generated supervision (the actual deployed setting).

Reviewers will reasonably ask "why is 40.85 PSNR a failure?". The honest answer: the residual is structured (periphery, articulation-specific) and predicts a worse failure under the deployment regime, but the multi-view D-NeRF number alone is not the right basis for the failure claim. **The SV4D-supervised D-NeRF and DyCheck rows are where the ill-posedness claim is actually demonstrated.**

## 4. Proposed W2 protocol (for discussion tomorrow)

To turn the W1 observation into a defensible "our hook fixes the failure" claim, we need at least three discriminating ablations:

| ablation | what it would rule out | predicted outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **A. shuffled part labels** (same hook, randomized SAM2 ids) | "Any extra regularizer helps" | Wins should collapse to baseline |
| **B. higher capacity baseline** (node_num 512 → 1024, uniform ARAP) | "Just needed more capacity" | Modest uniform gain across all scenes, including bouncingballs |
| **C. ours, full hook** | (the claim) | Wins concentrate on jumpingjacks > hellwarrior > standup, ~zero on bouncingballs |

Plus two diagnostics:
- **Per-radial-bin Δ-PSNR** vs. SC-GS-default — wins should localize to the high-distance bins (outer 25% of normalized radius). If wins are uniform across bins, the framing is wrong.
- **Per-scene Δ-PSNR correlation with articulation complexity** — the periphery/core ratio in §2 is itself a per-scene complexity proxy; Δ-PSNR should correlate with it.

**Sequencing proposal (W2, 2026-05-13 to 2026-05-18, lab box only):**
1. Day 1: ablation B (higher-capacity uniform baseline) on jumpingjacks — fastest, eliminates the "more capacity" objection before we commit to the full matrix.
2. Day 2: ablation C on jumpingjacks (the W1 gate scene per HANDOFF §6.4).
3. Days 3–4: ablations A, B, C × all 4 D-NeRF scenes, 3-way parallel.
4. Day 5: per-radial-bin Δ-PSNR aggregation; write up W2 report.

If by end of day 2 the jumpingjacks W2-C result does *not* visibly improve over W2-B on the radial profile, **stop and pivot** per HANDOFF §6.4 (Option B/C from the survey).

## 5. Deployment-regime experiment (W3, not blocking W2)

The actual claim — "articulation-aware ARAP recovers structure that uniform ARAP loses under generative supervision" — needs:
1. Take D-NeRF frame 0 GT for each scene → run SV4D 2.0 → 21-frame generated video.
2. Train SC-GS-default AND our hook on the *generated* video (not original D-NeRF train split).
3. Evaluate against D-NeRF held-out novel-view + novel-time GT.

This is what experiments.md §10.2 commits us to and is the experiment that supports the single-image-input framing in vwm_framing.md. The W2 results are necessary but not sufficient.

## 6. Risks I want feedback on tomorrow

1. **D-NeRF as primary benchmark** — defensible because it controls for camera, supervision, and pose. But reviewers will note SC-GS already nearly maxes it. Should we re-prioritize DyCheck (real monocular, where ill-posedness is unambiguous) earlier in the schedule? My read: keep D-NeRF as W2 because it's the only thing that lets us run the discriminating ablations cleanly (no real-world confounders), but bump DyCheck reproduction from "W3 nice-to-have" to "W3 must-have."
2. **The `articulation_only` ablation might not visibly win on jumpingjacks at 30K iters.** If SC-GS's uniform-ARAP gradient already pushes the field most of the way, our hook's marginal contribution may be PSNR-noise-floor small. Mitigation: train for longer (40K, matching SC-GS paper) on the W2 jumpingjacks row specifically, and report the radial-profile delta rather than aggregate PSNR.
3. **Two-tier compute plan still feasible** — lab box has handled W1 fine. W2 budget ~50 lab-hr (3-way parallel = ~17 hr wall clock). W3+ stays on RunPod per experiments.md §5.
4. **RigGS is the dangerous baseline.** They published 40.82 on jumpingjacks. We're at 40.85 on the same scene with SC-GS-default; their hook adds articulation. If our hook only moves us to 41.5, we don't beat their published number. Counter-narrative: we win on inter-part angular consistency (a metric they don't report), and on the single-image-input regime (which they can't run).
